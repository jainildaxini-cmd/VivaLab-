It behaves like an examiner, not a quiz app.

One question at a time

It waits for your answer before it says anything else, the way a real viva runs.

Follow-ups from your answer

Answer "a sequential circuit" and the next question is about the clock, not a random topic.

Difficulty that moves

Get two right and it goes harder. Struggle once and it drops back to the definition.

Weak topics tracked

Every answer is scored against your own material, so weak areas surface by topic.

No pointless repeats

Question sets are de-duplicated, so 40 questions mean 40 different questions.

Grounded, never invented

If the material does not support a question, VivaLab says so instead of making one up.

Questions students actually ask.

PDF, PPTX, DOCX, TXT, JPG, JPEG, PNG and audio files, plus a YouTube link or plain pasted text. PDF, PPTX, DOCX and TXT are read directly in your browser. Images go through OCR, and YouTube or audio need the server engine, which is where the paid plan comes in.

From your file. VivaLab extracts topics, definitions, formulas and procedures, then generates questions against those, and keeps the page or slide reference attached to each one. If your material does not support a topic, you will not be asked about it.

Reveal Answer mode shows a short model answer built from your material after you have attempted the question. Exam mode hides every answer until the session ends. It is a rehearsal tool, not a replacement for reading.

In this build, PDF, PPTX, DOCX, TXT and image processing all happen inside your browser. Nothing leaves your device for those formats. YouTube and audio need a server call, and that is clearly marked before you start.

It is subject-agnostic, so it works for any branch with lab, practical or project vivas: electronics, computer science, mechanical, civil, electrical, instrumentation and diploma streams. The sample viva uses Digital Electronics, which is a common second-year lab.
